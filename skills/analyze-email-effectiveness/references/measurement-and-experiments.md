# Measurement, diagnosis, and experiments

Use this reference to diagnose campaign/sequence results, define metrics, compare variants, or design a defensible test.

## Start with a metric contract

For every metric, record:

- event definition and source system;
- numerator, denominator and eligibility;
- unique person/account/message/event unit;
- event timestamp and time zone;
- attribution and maturity window;
- bot/privacy filtering;
- deduplication, retries and late-arriving-event behavior;
- exclusions and suppression logic;
- version/date of the definition.

Rates with the same name can be incomparable across ESPs or periods.

## Funnel definitions

State the denominator:

```text
send eligibility rate = eligible / evaluated population
send success rate = sent / eligible
acceptance rate = receiver accepted / attempted
delivery rate = provider-defined delivered / sent or accepted
hard-bounce rate = hard bounced / attempted
deferral rate = deferred attempts or messages / attempted
complaint rate = complaints / delivered, accepted, or provider-defined population
unsubscribe rate = unsubscribers / delivered
unique click-through rate = unique clickers / delivered
click-to-open rate = unique clickers / unique opens
reply rate = valid human replies / delivered
conversion rate = converters / eligible, delivered, clickers, or exposed
revenue per eligible/delivered = attributed revenue / stated denominator
retained value = value after refund/churn window / exposure
```

“Delivered” commonly means accepted by the destination server, not placed in the inbox. Provider complaint dashboards can use proprietary denominators; retain them rather than recomputing a misleading cross-provider average.

## Metric hierarchy by job

| Job | Primary evidence | Guardrails |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Security/service | successful safe completion, reduced failure/support | complaints, confusion, account harm |
| Notification/digest | useful downstream action or preference-adjusted value | opt-out, alert fatigue |
| Activation | target product value event | unsubscribe, support friction |
| Conversion | qualified mature conversion or retained value | refund, complaint, low-quality purchase |
| Nurture/editorial | later qualified action, useful reply or retained readership | fatigue, opt-out |
| Re-engagement | renewed meaningful activity | complaint, bounce, nonresponse |
| Feedback | valid representative response and closed loop | bias, over-surveying |
| Outreach | qualified human reply or meeting that progresses | opt-out, complaint, poor-fit meeting |

Optimize the recipient/business outcome, not the easiest telemetry.

## Open and click limitations

[Apple Mail Privacy Protection](https://www.apple.com/legal/privacy/data/en/mail-privacy-protection/) can download remote content regardless of engagement. Other image proxies create similar inflation and obscure device, location and timing. Opens remain useful for debugging some large changes only when client mix and collection method are stable.

Clicks can be generated by security scanners and link-preview systems. Look for:

- click immediately after acceptance or before plausible reading;
- every link clicked in rapid succession;
- repeated machine/network/user-agent patterns;
- click without session/product behavior;
- destination-domain security rewriting;
- provider/enterprise gateway concentration.

Prefer first-party downstream events, human replies, completed tasks and mature conversions. Document the filtering algorithm and preserve raw counts.

## Diagnostic segmentation

Start broad, then segment only where counts support it:

- mailbox provider/recipient domain;
- acquisition source and consent cohort;
- lifecycle state/trigger;
- new versus established recipient;
- sending domain, DKIM domain/selector, return-path, IP/pool and stream;
- template/message family/offer/destination;
- locale/time zone/device/client;
- customer value/plan/role;
- send day/hour and experiment cell.

Protect against multiple-comparison fishing. A small segment anomaly is a hypothesis, not a conclusion.

## Diagnostic patterns

- **Not attempted/sent:** eligibility, suppression, queue, workflow or data defect.
- **Low accepted/delivered:** invalid addresses, authentication, reputation, policy response, throttling or infrastructure before copy.
- **Accepted with provider deterioration:** complaints, source/volume changes, identity/pool, receiver rule or wantedness.
- **Stable placement but weak reliable engagement:** relevance, expectation, envelope, timing, message or measurement.
- **Clicks but low conversion:** promise/destination continuity, form/login, performance, offer, qualification or attribution.
- **High conversion but weak retained value:** targeting, discount, misleading promise, fraud, refund or product fit.
- **High unsubscribe/complaint:** source, expectation, frequency, recognition, purpose mismatch or preference failure.
- **Strong aggregate, weak receiver/segment:** average masking; isolate provider/identity/cohort.
- **Sudden step change:** first locate concurrent operational/list/product/measurement releases.

List at least one rival explanation and the next evidence that separates it from the leading cause.

## Cohort and sequence analysis

For lifecycle mail:

- cohort by eligibility/start event, not send date alone;
- use mature windows so later entrants are not penalized;
- attribute mutually exclusive exposures where possible;
- report cumulative conversion and time-to-event;
- include recipients who exit, suppress or never receive later steps;
- distinguish incremental sequence value from conversions that would occur anyway;
- analyze frequency/cannibalization across campaigns.

For revenue, document currency, discounts, tax/shipping, refund/cancellation, subscription renewal and attribution model.

## Comparison validity

Before comparing A and B, align:

- eligibility, source and lifecycle state;
- randomization/exposure unit;
- receiver and geographic mix;
- send period/frequency and concurrent campaigns;
- offer, price, inventory, landing page and product state;
- metric definition, instrumentation and maturity window;
- bot/privacy handling;
- sample size and missing data.

If multiple factors changed, describe an observational comparison and confounding; do not attribute the result to copy.

## Run the deterministic helper

Compare two independent binomial outcomes:

```bash
python scripts/analyze_email_experiment.py compare \
  --control-successes 420 --control-total 10000 \
  --variant-successes 470 --variant-total 10000 \
  --confidence 0.95 --format json
```

Plan an approximate balanced fixed-horizon test:

```bash
python scripts/analyze_email_experiment.py plan \
  --baseline-rate 0.04 \
  --absolute-mde 0.005 \
  --confidence 0.95 \
  --power 0.80 \
  --format json
```

The helper provides:

- control and variant Wilson intervals;
- absolute and relative lift;
- unpooled normal confidence interval for the difference;
- pooled two-sided z-test and p-value;
- approximate per-arm sample size.

It assumes independent Bernoulli outcomes and a fixed design. Do not use it blindly for account-level clustering, repeated measures, tiny expected counts, ratio metrics, revenue, time-to-event, multi-arm/adaptive tests, CUPED or sequential monitoring. Use a statistician/appropriate method.

## Experiment design

Predefine:

1. decision and one primary hypothesis;
2. eligibility and exclusions;
3. randomization unit—usually user/account, not message;
4. control and one causal difference;
5. primary metric, denominator and maturity window;
6. harm/quality guardrails;
7. minimum effect worth acting on in absolute units;
8. baseline and sample-size/power method;
9. fixed duration/sample or valid sequential design;
10. missing-data, bot and attribution rules;
11. multiple-testing adjustment where applicable;
12. ship/reject/continue thresholds and follow-up.

Randomize before content selection, prevent cross-cell contamination and use intention-to-treat as the primary analysis unless a different estimand is justified.

## Common inference failures

- repeated peeking and stopping at first significance;
- declaring “no effect” from non-significance without power/interval;
- choosing the winning metric after results;
- testing many subject lines with no correction;
- randomizing messages while measuring correlated users/accounts;
- using opens as the only success outcome;
- excluding bounces/opt-outs differently by cell;
- comparing immature conversions;
- reporting relative lift without absolute impact;
- shipping a statistically detectable but commercially trivial effect;
- ignoring guardrail harm;
- novelty/seasonality or concurrent offer changes;
- Simpson’s paradox across receiver/source segments.

Statistical significance is not practical importance or proof of mechanism.

## Small audiences

When a test is underpowered:

- collect qualitative evidence;
- test a larger strategic change;
- use a controlled rollout with guardrails;
- pool repeated comparable cycles using a predeclared method;
- test at product/journey level;
- make a reversible decision under uncertainty.

Do not run a pseudo-experiment merely to produce a percentage.
