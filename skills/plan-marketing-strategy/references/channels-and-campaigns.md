# Channels and Campaigns

Use this reference to choose channels for fit, design a connected campaign, and avoid treating distribution as an afterthought.

## 1. Channel portfolio

Assign each selected channel a job:

- **Primary engine:** main repeatable path to the outcome.
- **Capture layer:** converts demand already created elsewhere.
- **Support layer:** supplies proof, education, retargeting, follow-up, or retention.
- **Experiment:** bounded test of an uncertain audience, message, offer, or channel.

Choose the portfolio as a system. Search may capture demand developed through PR, community, partners, or social. Content may support sales and lifecycle. Product experience may determine whether paid acquisition can ever be economical.

## 2. Selection criteria

Assess:

- audience presence and addressability;
- intent and awareness at the moment of contact;
- message and format fit;
- trust requirements and sales-cycle length;
- price, margin, repeat rate, LTV uncertainty, and payback tolerance;
- time to first signal and time to compounding value;
- inventory, auction, algorithm, or partner dependence;
- creative/content supply and refresh burden;
- internal skill, follow-up, and service capacity;
- measurement and incrementality;
- control, portability, and platform/policy risk;
- accessibility, consent, privacy, and legal constraints.

Verify current platform mechanics and rules from primary sources. Do not recommend a channel solely because competitors use it or a benchmark calls it popular.

## 3. Channel guide

| Channel family | Fits when | Core unit | Early signal | Common failure |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Search and discovery: SEO, local, app stores, marketplaces, AI answers | People express an existing need | Helpful page/listing answering a specific intent | Qualified impressions, discoverability, engagement | Producing generic volume disconnected from the offer |
| Content and editorial | The purchase needs education, trust, or repeated exposure | Insight, guide, case, tool, comparison, story | Consumption by ICP, saves, assisted actions | Publishing without distribution or product connection |
| Email and lifecycle | A permissioned or expected relationship exists | Triggered message or recurring value | Reliable clicks, replies, valued action, retention | Calendar blasts without state or suppression logic |
| Organic social | Audience attention and credible participation exist | Native post, conversation, series, or creator voice | Relevant engagement, profile/action quality | Cross-posting generic content or chasing reach alone |
| Community | Peer learning, identity, support, or contribution creates value | Interaction, ritual, resource, recognition | Repeat participation and member outcomes | Building an audience-shaped broadcast channel |
| Partnerships, referrals, affiliates, influencers | Another party has trust or complementary access | Joint value proposition and tracked handoff | Qualified referrals and partner activation | Misaligned incentives or rented endorsement |
| Paid search, social, display, native, video, audio, app, retail, sponsorship | Economics and follow-up can support paid reach | Audience-message-creative-offer combination | Qualified downstream action and learning rate | Scaling before conversion, retention, or tracking is credible |
| Outbound, ABM, and sales enablement | The target is identifiable and deal value supports research | Relevant account/contact hypothesis and useful outreach | Positive replies, qualified conversations, pipeline quality | Scraped volume, fabricated personalization, weak handoff |
| PR and earned media | The organization has a genuinely newsworthy or useful story | Verifiable angle, evidence, expert access | Relevant coverage, branded demand, referral quality | Treating a press release as news or measuring clips alone |
| Events and webinars | Live access, demonstration, or peer exchange helps the decision | Session and follow-up journey | Target attendance, participation, qualified follow-up | Optimizing registrations without attendance or next steps |
| Product-led, free tools, templates, integrations | The product can deliver value before purchase or spread through use | Useful experience with a natural next step | Value completion, qualified activation, sharing | Gating too early or building a tool unrelated to demand |
| Direct messaging, text, push, in-product | A permissioned or service relationship makes a timely prompt useful | State-triggered message with preference and exit logic | Desired state change and retained value | Interruptive volume, weak consent, or conflicting messages |
| Marketplaces, directories, and listings | Customers evaluate within a trusted catalog or ecosystem | Accurate native listing with a maintained handoff | Qualified discovery, activation, transaction, or referral | Bulk submission, stale facts, or paid placement presented as independent |

Channel defaults are hypotheses. Adapt to B2B/B2C, category, geography, accessibility, regulation, buying committee, and product maturity.

## 4. Campaign architecture

Define:

```text
Objective and audience
-> trigger/insight
-> promise and proof
-> offer
-> creative/content units
-> distribution
-> destination/experience
-> capture or action
-> follow-up and handoff
-> retained value
-> measurement and decision
```

Create a campaign brief containing:

- context, constraint, objective, baseline, and horizon;
- priority segment, trigger, awareness, job, objection, and exclusion;
- single-minded proposition, supporting messages, proof, and prohibited claims;
- offer, CTA, destination, expectation, and fallback path;
- channel roles, formats, frequency logic, and sequencing;
- asset list, owners, dependencies, review, localization, and QA;
- eligibility, consent, suppression, sales/product/support handoffs;
- event definitions, test design, guardrails, and decision date;
- risks, contingency, and post-campaign reuse.

Do not launch traffic to an unready destination or create leads the team cannot serve.

## 5. Content and message system

Give every content unit a job:

- create recognition of a problem or opportunity;
- explain a category or mechanism;
- provide proof or reduce risk;
- help evaluation;
- enable a first success;
- deepen use or retention;
- support a sales conversation;
- create advocacy or referral.

Map content to audience, stage, query or trigger, distribution, destination, CTA, and metric. Build from reusable source material when appropriate, but adapt each derivative to its native channel instead of mechanically resizing it.

Use a balanced evidence mix:

- customer voice and observed problems;
- product demonstration;
- data or original analysis;
- expert point of view;
- case evidence;
- objection handling;
- practical utility.

Avoid a calendar that exists only to maintain cadence.

## 6. Distribution plan

For every asset specify:

| Asset | Priority audience | Channel | Native format | Owner | Publish/send trigger | Amplification | Destination | Success signal | Reuse |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

Include owned, earned, partner, paid, sales, community, and product distribution only when each has a defined role. Budget for distribution and iteration at planning time.

Use sequencing:

1. Seed with the most informed or trusted audience.
2. Capture qualitative objections and confusion.
3. Repair message and destination.
4. Expand to the primary channel.
5. Add amplification only after the path works.

## 7. Operational readiness

Before activation verify:

- factual, brand, legal, consent, privacy, accessibility, and platform review;
- working links, forms, redirects, tracking, fallbacks, and preference controls;
- audience eligibility, exclusions, deduplication, frequency, and time zones;
- inventory, fulfillment, support, sales routing, SLAs, and crisis ownership;
- creative variations tied to real hypotheses;
- dashboard or query with defined denominators;
- rollback, pause, or budget-cap controls;
- responsible owner for daily monitoring and final readout.

Record what shipped, not just what was planned.
