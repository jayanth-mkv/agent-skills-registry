# Market, Model, and Launch Playbooks

Use this reference to adapt marketing strategy to business model, maturity, buying motion, market geography, and launch risk.

## 1. Select the planning lens

Do not begin with a generic funnel. Define:

- customer, user, buyer, beneficiary, and partner;
- value exchange and revenue or funding model;
- sales, product, service, and fulfillment motion;
- decision complexity and time;
- unit, margin, repeat, retention, and capacity;
- market maturity and category awareness;
- geography, language, regulation, accessibility, and infrastructure;
- current evidence and largest uncertainty.

Use two maps:

```text
customer progress path
business delivery and economic path
```

Marketing is viable only where both paths work.

## 2. Match stage to evidence

| Stage | Primary question | Useful evidence | Marketing priority |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Problem discovery | Does a specific audience need progress badly enough? | Interviews, behavior, workarounds, attempts, stakes | Narrow audience and problem; avoid scaling claims |
| Solution validation | Will the audience act on this approach and exchange? | Prototype, concierge, preorder, sales conversation, usage | Test promise, mechanism, offer, and delivery |
| Early product-market fit | Who repeatedly reaches and retains value? | Cohorts, repeat use, retention, referrals, willingness to pay | Improve value path and find reachable high-fit segments |
| Repeatable growth | Can one engine acquire and serve customers economically? | Channel cohorts, conversion, margin, capacity, incrementality | Operationalize engine, creative, lifecycle, and measurement |
| Expansion | Which segments, products, or markets add durable value? | Adoption, cross-sell, market evidence, serviceability | Test one adjacency with a dedicated value proposition |
| Maturity | How should the portfolio defend, renew, and reallocate? | Brand, share, penetration, retention, marginal return | Refresh demand, improve efficiency, innovate, simplify |
| Decline or disruption | Is the constraint demand, relevance, delivery, or economics? | Segment and cohort loss, category change, substitution | Repair, reposition, harvest, migrate, or exit explicitly |

Do not label a weak launch as product-market fit failure without checking reach, audience, offer, and delivery. Do not label temporary acquisition growth as fit without retained value.

## 3. Adapt by business model

### Self-serve software or digital subscription

Path:

```text
discover -> evaluate -> start -> first value -> repeated use
-> pay -> retain -> expand
```

Focus on product proof, time to value, trial or free experience, lifecycle state, paywall timing, cohort retention, and support-efficient growth.

### Sales-led or enterprise

Path:

```text
target -> engage -> qualify -> build consensus -> validate
-> procure -> implement -> adopt -> renew/expand
```

Focus on account triggers, multiple buying roles, category and business case, sales enablement, security and implementation evidence, pipeline definitions, and stakeholder adoption.

### Ecommerce, retail, or repeat-purchase product

Path:

```text
discover -> evaluate product -> buy -> receive -> use
-> repeat -> advocate
```

Focus on merchandising, product truth, creative, availability, total price, checkout, fulfillment, returns, first-use experience, repeat timing, margin, and inventory.

### Marketplace or multi-sided network

Path:

```text
acquire each side -> create local/category liquidity
-> match -> transact or interact safely -> repeat
```

Choose the constrained side, geography, or category. Measure match quality, time to match, fill, trust, leakage, repeat, and side-specific economics. Avoid acquiring broad demand before supply can fulfill it.

### Local, multi-location, or service-area business

Path:

```text
local discovery/referral -> trust -> contact/book/visit
-> delivery -> review/repeat/referral
```

Focus on accurate location and availability data, reputation, local proof, response speed, booking, service capacity, location-level measurement, and brand consistency.

### Professional or managed service

Path:

```text
discover/refer -> qualify -> diagnose -> propose -> close
-> deliver milestone -> retain/refer
```

Focus on expertise, problem fit, case evidence, qualification, scope, capacity, proposal, delivery quality, and referral. Do not generate leads beyond delivery capacity.

### Mobile app or game

Path:

```text
store/ad/referral -> install -> permission/setup -> first value
-> repeated use -> purchase/subscription -> retain
```

Focus on listing-to-product continuity, install quality, privacy permissions, first session, crashes, engagement value, monetization fairness, re-engagement, and cohort retention.

### Developer product or open ecosystem

Path:

```text
discover -> understand -> try -> successful build
-> team adoption -> production trust -> expansion/contribution
```

Focus on documentation, examples, quick start, reliability, community, integrations, transparent limits, technical proof, and organizational adoption. Respect usage terms, security, and contribution governance.

### Founder-led or bootstrapped product

Use direct customer access, founder expertise, fast learning, manual service, and a narrow community or demand signal as advantages. Limit channels and production burden to actual capacity. Separate public building from customer value, and do not use attention metrics as a substitute for product evidence or revenue quality.

### Media, education, membership, or community

Path:

```text
discover -> consume -> return -> subscribe/join
-> participate/learn -> renew/contribute
```

Focus on editorial or learning promise, habit, creator and member value, discoverability, completion or outcome, community health, and sustainable monetization.

### Nonprofit, public-interest, or behavior-change program

Path:

```text
reach -> understand -> trust -> enroll/act/donate
-> complete -> experience outcome -> sustain/advocate
```

Separate funder, donor, participant, beneficiary, and partner needs. Optimize for mission outcome and equitable access, not conversion alone.

### Hybrid

Map each motion separately, then show dependencies and conflicts. Do not average self-serve, sales-led, partner-led, and service economics into one funnel.

## 4. Design go-to-market

Write the go-to-market contract:

```markdown
## Market and timing
## Priority segment and disqualifiers
## Job, trigger, alternatives, and stakes
## Product readiness and delivered value
## Position, promise, proof, and category education
## Offer, price, packaging, and purchase path
## Distribution advantage and primary engine
## Sales, product, service, partner, and support motion
## Activation, retention, and expansion path
## Measurement, learning agenda, and economics
## Capability, budget, dependencies, risks, and non-goals
```

Choose a beachhead where urgency, fit, access, proof, and serviceability reinforce one another. Define how learning from the beachhead will or will not transfer.

For a new category, allocate work to problem recognition, category explanation, risk reduction, and reference customers. For a mature category, allocate work to availability, meaningful differentiation, proof, switching, and memory.

## 5. Plan a launch

Use launch only when there is a real change worth coordinating. A launch is a sequence, not one announcement.

### Readiness

- product or service works for the promised use;
- priority audience and offer are clear;
- proof, demo, support, fulfillment, and sales are ready;
- destinations, tracking, lifecycle, and incident ownership work;
- claims, rights, accessibility, localization, and policy review are complete.

### Sequence

1. **Private learning:** validate with high-context users and fix severe failures.
2. **Proof building:** capture objections, outcomes, demonstrations, and operational load.
3. **Audience preparation:** educate, seed partners and contributors, and ready owned channels.
4. **Release:** coordinate message, experience, channels, support, and monitoring.
5. **Follow-through:** answer questions, repair gaps, retarget or nurture appropriately, and enable sales.
6. **Readout:** compare outcome and guardrails, preserve learning, and choose the next release.

Define launch tiers by change significance and risk. A small feature update should not require the production burden of a new category launch.

Create a run sheet with asset, owner, dependency, approval, release time, monitoring, rollback, and follow-up. Do not fabricate countdowns, waitlists, availability, endorsements, or newsworthiness.

## 6. Enter a new market or segment

Validate:

- problem and willingness to act in the new context;
- category language, alternatives, trust, and decision roles;
- accessible audience and channel economics;
- product, service, support, payment, tax, fulfillment, and infrastructure fit;
- legal, privacy, consent, claims, accessibility, and sector requirements;
- language, cultural meaning, imagery, units, currency, dates, and pricing;
- local partners, proof, and escalation;
- risk to current customers, channels, and brand.

Distinguish translation, localization, and market strategy. Start with a bounded market cell and explicit evidence gates. Do not copy a winning campaign into a new locale by translation alone.

## 7. Handle brand and portfolio change

For rebranding, repositioning, domain change, product consolidation, or portfolio expansion:

- state the business reason and customer impact;
- inventory names, assets, pages, listings, campaigns, integrations, contracts, and support content;
- define brand architecture and migration rules;
- preserve recognition, discovery, links, permissions, and customer access;
- communicate internally before external release;
- sequence high-risk technical and commercial dependencies;
- monitor confusion, trust, support, traffic, conversion, and partner impact;
- keep correction and rollback paths.

Do not use a visual rebrand to avoid a value, product, reputation, or category problem.

## 8. Build the capability plan

Map required capabilities rather than copying an ideal org chart:

| Capability | Current strength | Required level | Owner | Build, contract, partner, or defer | Evidence date |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

Consider research, positioning, brand, content, creative, discovery, paid media, lifecycle, sales, customer success, analytics, operations, product, engineering, finance, legal, accessibility, localization, and support.

Sequence:

1. outcome-critical owner;
2. minimum evidence and measurement;
3. product, offer, proof, and journey readiness;
4. primary distribution engine;
5. follow-up, retention, and service capacity;
6. supporting channels and automation;
7. specialization only when volume and complexity justify it.

## 9. Apply stop and scale rules

Before a test, define:

- premise and evidence;
- minimum credible effort;
- earliest valid signal and mature outcome;
- quality, trust, margin, capacity, and segment guardrails;
- continue, change, stop, and scale thresholds;
- owner and decision date.

Stop or redesign when the audience does not experience value, the channel cannot reach them responsibly, economics fail across plausible ranges, service capacity breaks, or material trust and policy risks cannot be controlled.

Scale only when:

- the customer path delivers value;
- the constraint has shifted from premise uncertainty to execution capacity;
- measurement is decision-useful;
- creative, content, sales, product, support, and fulfillment can sustain volume;
- marginal performance and customer quality remain acceptable;
- concentration and platform risks have explicit controls.
