# Outbound, Sales, and Revenue Operations

Use this reference to connect legitimate account discovery and outreach to useful sales conversations, reliable handoffs, pipeline learning, and customer value.

## 1. Confirm the motion and boundary

Use outbound when:

- the target organization or buyer can be identified from legitimate signals;
- the problem and trigger are specific enough to make contact useful;
- deal value and fit justify research and human follow-up;
- the sender has a lawful, policy-compliant basis for the channel and data;
- sales can respond, qualify, and serve the resulting demand.

Do not use outbound to compensate for undefined fit, undifferentiated value, a scraped mass list, or insufficient sales capacity.

Define:

```text
priority account -> trigger and problem hypothesis
-> relevant person or buying role -> useful reason for contact
-> proportionate next step -> qualified conversation
-> evaluation -> customer value
```

## 2. Select accounts and contacts

Build an account model:

- organization type, size, location, maturity, and operating model;
- relevant technology, process, regulation, event, or change;
- problem intensity and cost;
- solution fit, disqualifiers, and serviceability;
- expected value and buying complexity;
- relationship, partner, customer, product, or intent signals;
- timing and confidence.

Use demand signals such as a relevant hiring pattern, public initiative, product or policy change, expansion, new leadership, technology change, content interaction, event participation, or direct request. Verify the event and explain why it changes relevance; do not present surveillance as personalization.

Map roles:

- user and affected team;
- champion;
- economic buyer;
- technical or operational evaluator;
- security, legal, finance, procurement, or compliance reviewer;
- executive sponsor;
- blocker and likely reason;
- existing relationship owner.

Do not contact every person in an organization. Choose the role able to evaluate or route the issue, and suppress parallel outreach that creates a coordinated pile-on.

## 3. Build compliant data operations

For every field record:

- business purpose;
- origin and collection date;
- confidence and verification status;
- permitted use and jurisdiction constraints;
- owner, access, retention, update, and deletion;
- suppression and objection status.

Collect the minimum data needed for relevance, delivery, routing, and response. Verify identity using current public business information or authorized systems. Keep inferred attributes separate from verified facts.

Quality checks:

- account and domain match;
- active role and appropriate seniority;
- valid business contact path;
- no sensitive or unnecessary personal data;
- duplicates and existing ownership resolved;
- customers, partners, competitors, job candidates, complainants, and opted-out people handled correctly;
- suppression synchronized across senders and tools.

Do not buy or scrape personal contact lists, evade access controls, use leaked data, enrich sensitive characteristics, or continue after an objection.

## 4. Design account-based plays

Choose a tier based on value and research capacity:

| Tier | Treatment | Appropriate when |
| --- | --- | --- |
| One-to-one | Account-specific research, message, assets, and coordinated roles | Few high-value accounts with distinct needs |
| One-to-few | Segment or trigger play with shared evidence and events | Accounts share a strong situation and buying motion |
| One-to-many | Scaled but relevant content and outreach | Fit can be verified with limited customization |

Write an account plan:

```markdown
## Desired account outcome
## Fit, trigger, and evidence
## Problem and value hypothesis
## Buying roles and relationships
## Current alternative and likely objections
## Message, proof, and useful offer
## Touch sequence and owners
## Content, event, partner, and paid support
## Qualification and exit rules
## Metrics, review date, and next decision
```

Coordinate paid, events, content, partners, product signals, and sales only when each adds value. Do not call ordinary high-volume targeting account-based marketing.

For coordinated paid and outbound treatment:

- create one account/contact eligibility and suppression view;
- assign experimental cells before either channel activates;
- define precedence for customer, active opportunity, partner, opt-out, sensitive, and sales-owned states;
- share campaign/play identifiers without exposing personal data;
- cap cross-channel frequency and avoid simultaneous duplicate asks;
- log contamination, organic exposure, account movement, and manual overrides;
- reconcile sourced, influenced, and incremental reporting under one decision owner.

Do not let paid and sales teams independently claim the same account outcome or invalidate a holdout through untracked outreach.

## 5. Write outreach and sequences

Each message should answer:

1. Who is contacting the recipient?
2. Why this recipient or organization?
3. What observed fact makes the issue timely?
4. What useful hypothesis, evidence, resource, or offer is provided?
5. What small next step is requested?
6. How can the recipient decline or redirect?

Use:

```text
verified context -> relevant problem hypothesis -> useful value
-> proof or reason to believe -> proportionate question
```

Personalize only with accurate, decision-relevant context. State uncertainty when the need is a hypothesis. Keep subjects, sender identity, routing, and threading truthful.

Plan a sequence by distinct jobs:

- introduce relevance;
- add evidence, a useful point of view, or a resource;
- address the most likely objection;
- offer an alternative path or ask for routing;
- close respectfully and suppress when appropriate.

Coordinate email, call, social, event, partner, and direct-mail touches by recipient expectation. Do not create fake reply threads, false deadlines, false referrals, deceptive calendar invitations, automated harassment, or messages that imply research never performed.

## 6. Create sales enablement

Build assets for a specific conversation:

| Asset | Job |
| --- | --- |
| Discovery guide | Understand situation, impact, current process, urgency, and decision |
| Role or segment one-pager | Make relevance, value, proof, and next step easy to share |
| Demo plan | Show the workflow and outcome that answer the buyer's questions |
| Objection guide | Provide evidence, questions, limits, and escalation—not scripted pressure |
| Comparison guide | Support fair tradeoffs against real alternatives |
| Customer story | Demonstrate context, intervention, outcome, and limits |
| Business case | Model value and cost with transparent assumptions and sensitivity |
| Security or implementation brief | Explain requirements, ownership, risk, and rollout |
| Proposal | Confirm scope, outcomes, responsibilities, price, terms, and decision path |
| Mutual action plan | Coordinate milestones, owners, evidence, dependencies, and dates |

Maintain approved claims, current product facts, pricing, case permissions, and expiration dates. Give sales a way to report missing evidence and new objections.

## 7. Define lifecycle, qualification, and routing

Model observable states:

```text
identified -> engaged -> accepted for follow-up -> qualified
-> evaluating -> committed -> onboarded -> successful
-> renewed/expanded or closed/lost/recycled
```

For each state specify entry, owner, required evidence, permitted actions, exit, service level, and next destination.

Use qualification to decide fit and next help, not to inflate scores. Combine:

- problem and desired outcome;
- fit and disqualifiers;
- authority and buying roles;
- timing and compelling event;
- value and economics;
- technical, security, service, or operational feasibility;
- observed engagement when reliable.

Define accepted, rejected, duplicate, disqualified, recycled, no-response, and nurture handling. Route on the minimum data required. Preserve context, consent, and recent interactions without exposing unnecessary personal information.

## 8. Operate pipeline and feedback

Define sales stages by buyer progress, not seller activity. Require clear exit evidence. Audit:

- ownership, territories, queues, and response time;
- duplicate account/contact/opportunity handling;
- stage age and stalled paths;
- source and campaign capture;
- close, loss, no-decision, and recycle reasons;
- forecast category definitions;
- proposal, discount, security, procurement, and deal approvals;
- onboarding and customer-success handoff;
- retention and expansion feedback.

Create a closed loop:

```text
marketing hypothesis -> sales conversation -> objection/outcome
-> product/customer result -> message, targeting, offer, and roadmap update
```

Review recordings, notes, and transcripts only with appropriate notice, access, retention, and redaction. Sample for quality; do not treat automated summaries as ground truth.

## 9. Measure quality and economics

Track:

- eligible accounts and verified contacts;
- delivery, objection, opt-out, complaint, and suppression health;
- positive or useful replies;
- accepted meetings and attendance;
- qualified opportunities and account progression;
- pipeline value with stage-quality caveats;
- win, loss, no-decision, sales cycle, contract value, margin, and implementation capacity;
- activation, retention, expansion, and customer fit by source;
- research, data, sender, sales, and fulfillment cost.

Use cohort and account-level views. Separate sourced, influenced, and causal impact. A meeting is not pipeline; pipeline is not revenue; revenue is not retained customer value.

## 10. Complete safety and launch review

Verify:

- legitimate business purpose and current jurisdiction/channel requirements;
- truthful identity, subject, content, sender configuration, and contact reason;
- data rights, minimization, security, access, retention, and deletion;
- account/contact accuracy, duplicates, ownership, and suppression;
- frequency, sequence exit, objection, unsubscribe, and complaint handling;
- sales capacity, routing, service levels, calendars, and fallback;
- claims, case evidence, price, product availability, and competitor facts;
- provider rules and technical delivery requirements;
- monitoring, pause authority, incident owner, and audit record.

Drafting lists or sequences does not authorize acquisition of data, enrollment, sending, calling, or production-system changes. Obtain explicit authority for those actions.
