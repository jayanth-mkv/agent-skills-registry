# Marketing Surfaces and Assets

Use this reference to choose, design, write, or audit the pages and components that connect a marketing promise to a customer decision.

## Contents

1. Choose the surface
2. Plan the page portfolio
3. Write a surface brief
4. Design the decision path
5. Select components deliberately
6. Apply page-family playbooks
7. Build trust and accessibility
8. Validate before release

## 1. Choose the surface

Start with the customer's decision:

```text
entry context -> question -> evidence needed -> action
-> confirmation -> next experience
```

Choose the smallest surface that can support that decision. Do not create a new page when an existing maintained page can serve the audience without mixing incompatible intents.

Define:

- audience, situation, awareness, and source;
- page job and one primary decision;
- promise, proof, offer, and decisive objection;
- primary action, alternative path, and destination;
- information required before acting;
- discovery, campaign, sales, product, support, or legal dependencies;
- event definitions, guardrails, owner, and maintenance trigger.

## 2. Plan the page portfolio

| Family | Typical surfaces | Primary job |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Brand and corporate | Home, about, contact, careers, newsroom, media kit | Establish identity, relevance, legitimacy, and paths |
| Product and service | Overview, product, service, feature, category | Explain what is offered, for whom, and why it matters |
| Audience and outcome | Use case, solution, industry, role, company size, startup or enterprise | Connect an audience situation to a credible outcome |
| Evaluation | Pricing, packages, comparison, alternatives, integration, migration, customer story, showcase | Support tradeoffs, proof, fit, and risk reduction |
| Acquisition | Campaign landing, lead magnet, assessment, download, template, free tool, event, webinar, contest | Complete a defined value exchange |
| Learning | Article, blog hub, guide, resource hub, glossary, FAQ, documentation, API, podcast, video | Teach, solve, support discovery, and connect to progress |
| Partnership | Affiliate, referral, creator, education, partner, integration, press coverage | Explain mutual value, eligibility, and next steps |
| Commerce | Category, collection, product detail, offer, cart, checkout, shipping and return information | Help selection, purchase, fulfillment, and confidence |
| Account and product entry | Signup, login, download, install, trial, demo, onboarding handoff | Begin or resume a product experience |
| Utility and trust | Status, changelog, feedback, accessibility, privacy, cookie, terms, disclosure, refund | Set expectations, provide control, and handle essential information |
| Recovery | Not-found, unavailable, discontinued, failed payment, form error | Explain the state and provide a safe next path |

Do not use feature, use-case, and solution pages as interchangeable keyword variants. Features explain capabilities; use cases explain a situation and workflow; solutions explain an outcome for a segment or context.

Treat legal and policy surfaces as specialist-owned. Marketing may organize inputs and improve clarity, but must not invent obligations or represent unreviewed text as approved.

## 3. Write a surface brief

```markdown
# Surface brief
## Page job and primary decision
## Audience, entry context, and awareness
## Current state and evidence
## Promise, mechanism, and proof
## Offer, price or value exchange, and eligibility
## Objections and risk
## Primary action, destination, and next experience
## Required sections and content sources
## Brand, voice, visual, localization, and accessibility requirements
## Discovery, technical, analytics, and policy requirements
## Owner, reviewers, dependencies, and deadline
## Primary outcome, guardrails, and test plan
```

For a redesign, preserve working information, discovery equity, links, events, and customer paths unless evidence supports changing them. Separate visual preference from decision performance.

## 4. Design the decision path

Use a flexible argument:

1. Orient the visitor: what this is and whether it is relevant.
2. State the valuable outcome or task.
3. Explain how the offer or resource creates that outcome.
4. Show the product, service, method, or evidence.
5. Resolve the decisive objections and fit questions.
6. Present price, terms, requirements, or next-step effort clearly.
7. Ask for a proportionate action.
8. Confirm what happened and guide the next experience.

The first screen should make identity, relevance, and the primary path understandable without unsupported hype. Longer pages are justified by decision complexity, not a generic conversion rule.

Maintain message continuity from source to page:

```text
audience + promise + proof + offer + action
```

A campaign-specific landing page may narrow the message, but must not contradict the durable product truth.

## 5. Select components deliberately

| Component | Use when | Required quality |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hero or opening | The visitor needs fast orientation | Clear subject, relevance, promise, proof cue, and path |
| CTA | A next step is appropriate | Specific action, accurate destination, proportional commitment |
| Form or signup | Information is necessary to fulfill or qualify | Minimum fields, labels, purpose, consent, errors, recovery |
| Popup, banner, or modal | Timing or state makes an interruption useful | Relevant trigger, frequency, close control, focus management |
| Testimonial or customer evidence | Experience reduces uncertainty | Permission, accurate scope, identity rules, qualification |
| Trust signal | A specific risk needs reassurance | Verifiable, current, explained near the concern |
| Comparison table | Structured tradeoffs aid evaluation | Fair dimensions, readable mobile form, qualified claims |
| How-to or FAQ | Steps or recurring questions aid progress | Direct answers, complete context, crawlable and accessible content |
| Tabs or accordion | Grouped content reduces overload | Visible labels, keyboard support, stable deep links where needed |
| Navigation, breadcrumb, sidebar, or contents | Users need orientation across information | Logical hierarchy, descriptive labels, predictable behavior |
| Card, grid, list, carousel, or masonry | Multiple items need scanning | Choose for comparison and order; do not hide priority |
| Footer | Users need durable utility and trust paths | Contact, preferences, policies, navigation, locale, ownership |
| Social preview and share control | External sharing is likely | Accurate title, summary, image rights, and destination |

Do not add testimonials, badges, counters, urgency bars, or popups merely because a checklist recommends them. Each component must resolve a real question or enable a real action.

## 6. Apply page-family playbooks

### Home

- orient multiple important audiences without becoming a site map;
- establish category, promise, differentiation, proof, and primary paths;
- surface the most important products, use cases, or resources;
- help returning visitors reach common destinations quickly.

### Product, service, feature, solution, and use case

- define the relevant customer situation;
- show the workflow or mechanism rather than listing benefits without evidence;
- distinguish capability, outcome, and segment fit;
- connect supporting proof, integrations, limitations, and next step.

### Landing and lead-capture

- match the entry message and offer;
- remove unrelated choices without trapping the visitor;
- explain the value exchange, required data, delivery, and follow-up;
- preserve an alternative path for people who are interested but not ready.

### Pricing and packaging

- state the pricing logic, billing basis, inclusions, limits, and important terms;
- help visitors identify the suitable package;
- explain trial, demo, contact, discount, renewal, tax, or overage behavior where relevant;
- make comparison accessible and avoid hidden total-price surprises.

### Comparison and alternatives

- use real alternatives and decision criteria;
- state where another option fits better;
- support claims with current evidence;
- separate factual comparison from opinion;
- offer a path for uncertain visitors to evaluate directly.

### Customer story and showcase

- establish customer context, starting state, intervention, outcome, time frame, and limits;
- preserve the customer's voice and approval;
- distinguish measured results from interpretation;
- connect the story to a similar audience without claiming universality.

### Article, guide, glossary, documentation, and FAQ

- satisfy the learning or support job completely enough to be useful;
- state experience, method, sources, assumptions, and update date when material;
- use examples, visuals, steps, or reference tables where they improve understanding;
- provide a next step that follows naturally from the task.

### Template, tool, assessment, and download

- make the promised utility real;
- show inputs, output, limitations, privacy, and delivery;
- do not fabricate personalized findings;
- gate only when the exchange is clear and the ungated preview supports an informed choice.

### Signup, checkout, download, and install

- request only necessary information and permissions;
- explain costs, requirements, verification, and errors;
- preserve entered data safely where appropriate;
- confirm success and immediately connect to first value.

### Status, changelog, feedback, and recovery

- state current condition and impact plainly;
- provide time, ownership, or update expectation only when known;
- offer a working fallback;
- avoid promotional copy that obstructs recovery.

## 7. Build trust and accessibility

Check:

- semantic structure, headings, landmarks, reading order, labels, and instructions;
- keyboard operation, visible focus, target size, error identification, and recovery;
- text and interface contrast, zoom, reflow, captions, transcripts, and alternative text;
- reduced motion and avoidance of flashing or auto-play harm;
- clear language, consistent help, and accessible authentication;
- price, claim, review, endorsement, availability, and policy accuracy;
- consent, preference, privacy, data minimization, and sensitive-data handling;
- localization of meaning, units, dates, currencies, images, names, examples, and reading direction.

Do not use accessibility as a post-design checklist only. Include it in requirements, content, design, development, and acceptance testing.

## 8. Validate before release

Run four passes:

1. **Truth and fit:** audience, promise, proof, offer, price, eligibility, dates, permissions, and qualifications.
2. **Decision experience:** comprehension, hierarchy, objections, action, destination continuity, mobile behavior, and recovery.
3. **Technical quality:** links, forms, events, response codes, redirects, metadata, media, performance, security, and integrations.
4. **Trust and inclusion:** accessibility, consent, preferences, disclosures, localization, legal review, and harmful edge cases.

Test representative devices, input methods, locales, visitor states, empty and error states, and downstream handoffs. Record what shipped, baseline metrics, monitoring owner, rollback path, and review date.
