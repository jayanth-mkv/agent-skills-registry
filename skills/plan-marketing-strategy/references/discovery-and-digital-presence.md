# Discovery and Digital Presence

Use this reference to make an organization discoverable where customers actively look, while connecting visibility to a useful destination and a measurable customer outcome.

## 1. Define the discovery job

Choose the surface by audience behavior:

| Customer behavior | Discovery surface | Useful destination |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Expresses a question, problem, or category need | General or specialist search | Page that resolves the intent and offers a proportionate next step |
| Looks for a nearby provider | Local search and maps | Accurate location or service-area experience |
| Looks for an app | App marketplace | Truthful listing connected to successful first use |
| Compares vendors or tools | Review, directory, marketplace, comparison, community | Evidence-rich evaluation path |
| Asks an answer engine or assistant | Search- and answer-enabled content | Accessible, indexable, attributable primary information |
| Browses a platform or catalog | Marketplace or platform discovery | Complete listing, useful media, availability, and fulfillment |

Define the business outcome, audience, intent, destination, conversion or value event, and retention or quality guardrail. Visibility without a useful next experience is not success.

## 2. Research demand and intent

Combine:

- first-party site search, support, sales, product, and conversion data;
- query and performance data with defined dates and locations;
- direct customer language from interviews and observed behavior;
- competitor and result-page analysis;
- marketplace, community, review, and local-language patterns;
- seasonality, demand maturity, and zero-click behavior.

Classify intent by the progress sought, not a single keyword label:

- understand or solve;
- locate or navigate;
- compare or evaluate;
- buy, book, install, visit, or contact;
- use, troubleshoot, renew, or expand.

Cluster queries only when one page can satisfy the same underlying job. Separate pages when audience, intent, offer, proof, or next action materially differs. Record demand estimates as ranges and note tool, sampling, geography, and recency limitations.

## 3. Audit technical discoverability

Inspect in dependency order:

1. **Access:** server response, authentication, network rules, crawler access, and accidental blocking.
2. **Discovery:** internal links, navigation, submitted URL lists, feeds, supported update-notification methods, and orphaned content.
3. **Rendering:** meaningful content and links available to relevant clients and crawlers.
4. **Index control:** index directives, duplicate handling, preferred URLs, pagination, filters, parameters, and soft errors.
5. **Site changes:** redirects, status codes, domain or path moves, staging controls, and removal procedures.
6. **Experience:** mobile behavior, performance, stability, accessibility, security, and intrusive interruptions.
7. **Machine-readable data:** syntax, eligibility, visible-content match, completeness, and current feature support.
8. **International delivery:** language/region alternatives, locale routing, currency, and fallback behavior.

Separate crawl, render, index, rank, appearance, and conversion problems. A submitted URL list does not guarantee indexing; valid machine-readable data does not guarantee a special appearance.

For an audit, report evidence, impact, affected scope, reproduction method, confidence, owner, and verification step. Prioritize blockers and high-value templates before isolated cosmetic issues.

## 4. Design information and page architecture

Map:

```text
audience need -> topic/entity -> page job -> URL and template
-> navigation/internal links -> destination -> maintained owner
```

Use a page inventory with:

| Page or pattern | Audience/intent | Unique value | Primary next step | Index state | Owner | Review trigger |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

Keep important pages reachable through contextual links. Use descriptive link language and clear hierarchy. Avoid creating separate pages whose only difference is a swapped keyword or location name.

Choose domain, subdomain, directory, and multi-site structures based on customer clarity, brand architecture, operational ownership, localization, and technical maintenance. Do not assume a structural change creates demand.

## 5. Improve page and content quality

For each page:

- state the page's audience, intent, and decision;
- provide original information, experience, analysis, data, demonstration, or utility;
- answer the main need clearly before expanding;
- use accurate titles, headings, summaries, media descriptions, and social previews;
- show who or what is responsible when readers need to assess trust;
- support material claims and distinguish observation from inference;
- make price, availability, location, date, and policy information current;
- connect naturally to the next useful page or action;
- ensure images, video, audio, and interactive tools have usable text alternatives;
- define maintenance, consolidation, redirect, or removal rules.

Do not target a word count, keyword density, or publishing cadence as a quality goal. Do not refresh dates without a substantive update.

## 6. Support search and answer experiences

Build on durable fundamentals:

- technically accessible and eligible pages;
- clear, visible text for important facts;
- original, specific, well-supported information;
- coherent entities, terminology, and relationships;
- useful images, video, tables, examples, and comparisons;
- accurate machine-readable data that matches the page;
- strong internal discovery and a satisfying post-click experience;
- current business, product, location, and commerce information.

For complex questions, provide direct answers plus enough context, method, limitations, and evidence to support trust. Structure content for human comprehension; answer systems may extract from it, but extraction is not guaranteed.

Do not promise inclusion or citation. Do not invent special markup, hidden text, or machine-only files as a universal requirement. Do not create large volumes of paraphrased content to manipulate visibility. Verify current controls and reporting capabilities before giving implementation instructions.

## 7. Govern scaled discovery

Use template- and data-driven publishing only when the combination creates distinct customer value. Require:

- a legitimate repeating need;
- authoritative and maintainable data;
- a page model with meaningful unique information;
- explicit inclusion and exclusion rules;
- useful handling of sparse, missing, or conflicting data;
- editorial, factual, technical, accessibility, and duplication checks;
- navigation that does not generate infinite spaces;
- controlled release and indexation;
- performance, conversion-quality, and complaint monitoring;
- consolidation, correction, and removal paths.

Pilot a representative sample before scaling. Inspect generated output, not only the template. Stop expansion when pages become interchangeable, unsupported, misleading, or operationally unmaintainable.

Free tools, calculators, directories, templates, and glossaries should complete a real job. Do not fake functionality or gate the useful result without a clear value exchange.

## 8. Operate local, app, marketplace, and directory presence

### Local presence

- confirm eligibility and authorized ownership;
- keep business name, address or service area, phone, hours, categories, and availability accurate;
- align real-world and digital identity;
- create location pages only when each serves a real location or service need;
- request honest feedback without incentives that distort sentiment;
- respond to reviews without exposing personal information;
- monitor duplicates, edits, closures, and support escalation.

### App presence

- confirm store, category, age, territory, account, review-access, and current submission requirements;
- align name, category, description, media, privacy information, permissions, and claims with actual functionality;
- research category and query language by locale;
- localize meaning, screenshots, previews, support, price, and product availability—not metadata alone;
- show the product experience honestly in screenshots and previews;
- use supported listing variants or page experiments only when each maps to a real audience hypothesis;
- map ad/listing -> store -> install -> open -> onboarding -> core value, with a tested fallback at every step;
- test ordinary, deferred, and re-engagement links across installed/not-installed, signed-in/out, locale, device, and error states;
- preserve only necessary campaign context through the link and avoid personal or sensitive values in parameters;
- explain a permission in context, request it when its value is understandable, and preserve a useful path when declined;
- reconcile store, privacy-preserving aggregate, partner, owned event, and experiment reporting without double counting;
- test listing elements with downstream activation and retention guardrails;
- monitor ratings, reviews, crashes, load failures, uninstall or inactivity, support, install quality, and policy status.

### Marketplaces and directories

- select placements for audience fit, trust, referral quality, or integration value;
- create a truthful, complete, placement-native listing;
- tailor positioning without changing facts;
- route traffic to the most relevant maintained destination;
- track referral, qualification, activation, and maintenance cost;
- decline paid placement or review arrangements that obscure the commercial relationship.

Do not automate bulk submissions where accuracy, category fit, review, or account ownership cannot be maintained.

## 9. Build authority without manipulation

Earn discovery through:

- useful original research, tools, datasets, demonstrations, and documentation;
- expert contributions with real review and accountability;
- customer and partner stories with permission and qualification;
- relevant public relations, partnerships, communities, and events;
- consistent organization, product, author, and location information;
- links and references given because the resource helps the audience.

Reject link schemes, fabricated citations, impersonation, review manipulation, expired-site abuse, hidden redirects, reputation laundering, and publishing on external properties primarily to borrow authority. Evaluate off-site opportunities by audience value and editorial legitimacy, not a single authority score.

When auditing inbound links, examine relevance, editorial context, placement, pattern, destination, traffic, and control. A suspicious-looking link is not proof of harm. Use removal or platform-specific remediation only when evidence and current official guidance justify it; do not create unnecessary risk from indiscriminate link cleanup.

## 10. Monitor, migrate, and recover

Maintain a recurring view of:

- access, indexing, preferred-page, and machine-readable-data errors;
- impressions, qualified visits, actions, and retained value by intent and page type;
- branded and non-branded demand;
- local, app, marketplace, image, video, and answer-surface visibility where measurable;
- content decay, factual staleness, broken links, and orphaned pages;
- template anomalies, duplicate growth, and low-value inventory;
- manual actions, account enforcement, security incidents, and sudden tracking changes.

For a migration:

1. inventory current URLs, traffic, links, conversions, and ownership;
2. define one-to-one destinations and exceptions;
3. preserve critical content, metadata, internal paths, and analytics;
4. test redirects, response codes, preferred URLs, locale signals, and submitted URL lists;
5. release in a controlled window with rollback ownership;
6. monitor old and new paths, fix misses, and retain redirects long enough for users and systems.

For a decline, compare affected page groups, queries, devices, locations, dates, releases, demand, competitors, policy notices, and instrumentation. Do not label every loss an algorithm penalty or respond with indiscriminate deletion.
