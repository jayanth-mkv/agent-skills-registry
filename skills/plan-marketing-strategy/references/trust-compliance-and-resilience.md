# Trust, Compliance, and Resilience

Use this reference to identify marketing risk, build approval and control into execution, and respond when customer trust or live operations fail. This is operational guidance, not legal advice.

## 1. Classify the risk

Assess:

- audience vulnerability and sensitivity;
- health, finance, employment, housing, education, political, civic, safety, or other regulated context;
- reach, spend, permanence, and reversibility;
- strength and novelty of claims;
- personal data type, source, purpose, and sharing;
- automated decision or action impact;
- contract, price, promotion, billing, or fulfillment consequence;
- creator, partner, customer, employee, or outside-party dependence;
- jurisdiction, language, channel, and platform;
- accessibility and unequal-impact risk.

Assign:

| Level | Example characteristics | Minimum control |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Routine | Low reach, reversible, established facts, no sensitive data | Owner review and standard QA |
| Elevated | Paid reach, customer data, promotion, testimonial, new claim | Specialist review, evidence, monitoring, pause path |
| High | Sensitive category, vulnerable audience, automated eligibility, major price/policy change | Qualified legal or domain review, executive authority, staged release |
| Critical | Immediate safety, privacy, financial, security, or public-trust exposure | Stop, contain, escalate, document, and communicate under incident leadership |

When requirements conflict or are uncertain, pause the affected part and seek qualified review. Do not convert a checklist into a legal conclusion.

## 2. Substantiate claims and proof

Maintain a claim record:

| Claim | Exact meaning | Evidence | Scope and qualification | Audience/channel | Approver | Review date |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

Check:

- evidence exists before publication;
- the evidence supports the ordinary interpretation, not a narrower hidden meaning;
- sample, method, time, comparison, and uncertainty are represented;
- demonstration reflects the actual available product;
- typical and exceptional outcomes are not confused;
- superlatives and comparative claims have current support;
- price, savings, availability, scarcity, and deadline are real;
- health, performance, environmental, safety, and financial claims receive specialist review;
- machine-readable data, captions, images, and footnotes do not contradict the main message.

Place qualifications where people encounter the claim, in readable and understandable form. A disclosure cannot repair a fundamentally misleading headline or visual.

## 3. Govern endorsements, reviews, and partners

Require:

- a genuine experience or valid expert basis;
- accurate representation of the contributor's view;
- clear disclosure of payment, gift, employment, family, affiliate, or other material connection;
- disclosure close to the endorsement and present in the same language and medium;
- permission for name, likeness, content, editing, duration, placement, and paid amplification;
- monitoring, correction, and termination processes;
- current platform and jurisdiction review.

For reviews:

- solicit honest feedback, not positive feedback;
- do not create, buy, suppress, condition, or selectively alter sentiment;
- do not ask employees or related parties to appear independent;
- disclose incentives and avoid incentive structures that distort aggregate ratings;
- preserve a way to report fraud and resolve support problems separately.

For creators, affiliates, partners, and agencies, the organization retains responsibility for instructions, claims, monitoring, and correction. A contract does not replace operational oversight.

## 4. Protect privacy, consent, and preferences

Create a data map:

```text
person and context -> data collected -> purpose and valid basis
-> system and access -> use and sharing -> retention/deletion
-> preference, objection, access, correction, and incident path
```

Use:

- purpose limitation and data minimization;
- clear notice at the relevant moment;
- valid consent where required, without preselection or coercion;
- separate choices for materially different purposes;
- identity, access, encryption, audit, vendor, and export controls;
- retention tied to need;
- deletion and suppression that propagate to downstream systems;
- special treatment for children, sensitive traits, precise location, health, finance, communications, and inferred data.

Before using externally supplied data, verify it was collected and may be shared and used for the intended marketing purpose. Do not assume public availability creates unrestricted permission.

Do not expose personal data in URLs, campaign names, prompts, screenshots, dashboards, creative, or general briefs. Hashing or pseudonymization reduces some exposure but does not establish a valid purpose.

## 5. Control direct communications

For email, text, push, calls, direct mail, and automated messaging, identify:

- sender and recipient locations;
- relationship and reasonable expectation;
- promotional, transactional, service, political, fundraising, or other message class;
- data and contact origin;
- identity and sender authentication;
- required consent, notice, address, disclosure, preference, and opt-out;
- frequency, quiet hours, suppression, and retention;
- provider and carrier rules;
- reply, complaint, support, and abuse handling.

Keep sender identity, headers, subject, message, destination, and intent accurate. Make preference and unsubscribe controls visible, functional, and promptly honored. Separate essential service communication from unrelated promotion.

For high-volume email, verify current provider authentication, alignment, transport, format, spam-rate, and one-step unsubscribe requirements before sending. Monitor accepted, rejected, complaint, unsubscribe, and reputation signals. Do not promise inbox placement or evade filtering.

For cold outreach, require narrow relevance, a legitimate business reason, truthful personalization, low frequency, objection handling, and a working suppression system. Refuse purchased or scraped lists, deceptive threads, rotating identities, or provider-limit evasion.

## 6. Review promotions, commerce, and regulated contexts

Before a discount, contest, giveaway, referral reward, affiliate offer, free trial, subscription, automatic renewal, fee, guarantee, shipping promise, or refund policy:

- define eligibility, territory, dates, inventory, odds or selection, value, and exclusions;
- state total price and material terms before commitment;
- verify ordinary and discounted reference prices;
- make cancellation, return, refund, renewal, and support paths operable;
- prevent duplicate, self-referral, bot, account, payment, and coupon abuse;
- align ad, landing, checkout, receipt, fulfillment, and service behavior;
- obtain qualified review for current consumer, tax, gaming, promotion, subscription, and sector rules.

Do not use hidden fees, drip pricing, prechecked additions, fake countdowns, false scarcity, dark patterns, obstructed cancellation, or a “free” claim that conceals a material obligation.

For sensitive categories or audiences, verify targeting restrictions, age and location controls, required authorizations, disclaimers, prohibited claims, archival requirements, and landing-page rules before planning distribution.

## 7. Build accessible and inclusive experiences

Set an applicable accessibility target and verify the current standard or contractual requirement. Include:

- semantic structure and meaningful order;
- keyboard access and visible focus;
- sufficient contrast and non-color cues;
- text resizing, reflow, target size, and orientation support;
- labels, instructions, autocomplete, and helpful error recovery;
- captions, transcripts, audio description where needed, and useful alternative text;
- pause and motion controls;
- accessible authentication and avoidance of unnecessary repeated entry;
- language, reading direction, plain language, and consistent help.

Test with automated checks, keyboard-only use, representative assistive technology, zoom/reflow, and human review. Automated checks alone do not establish accessibility.

Review segmentation, imagery, examples, language, data, models, and delivery for exclusion, stereotyping, proxy discrimination, and unequal access. Do not remove a high-value audience merely because the current experience is inaccessible; include remediation in the plan.

## 8. Assure AI-assisted work

For research, strategy, copy, creative, personalization, scoring, summarization, generation, and automation:

- state the role of the model and the human decision owner;
- use only data appropriate for the task;
- separate supplied facts, verified findings, inference, and generated hypotheses;
- verify names, quotes, prices, policies, specifications, claims, and citations;
- inspect for fabricated customers, experts, evidence, interfaces, results, and endorsements;
- check originality, rights, likeness, trademark, voice, and confidential information;
- test representative languages, segments, edge cases, and accessibility;
- require approval before publication, contact enrollment, spend, pricing, eligibility, or production changes;
- log consequential inputs, outputs, reviews, changes, and rollback;
- provide a correction and complaint path.

Do not present synthetic customer research as observed research. Do not imitate a living person's identity or imply their endorsement. Do not hide automation when disclosure is reasonably necessary for trust, consent, or policy.

## 9. Protect rights, brands, and platforms

Verify rights for:

- copy, images, video, audio, music, fonts, data, code, templates, and screenshots;
- names, logos, trademarks, product packaging, and comparative use;
- customer, employee, creator, and event participant likeness or voice;
- editing, translation, derivative work, placement, territory, duration, and paid use.

Maintain:

- authorized brand assets and usage rules;
- account ownership, access, recovery, and two-person controls for high-impact changes;
- impersonation and infringement monitoring;
- brand-suitability and placement exclusions;
- current platform policy review and appeal owner;
- evidence capture and escalation for abuse.

Do not evade enforcement by creating replacement accounts, cloaking destinations, disguising prohibited content, or changing surface wording while preserving the same harmful practice.

## 10. Prepare incident and correction response

Prepare for:

- unsupported or incorrect claim;
- wrong price, promotion, availability, or fulfillment;
- privacy, consent, preference, or data exposure;
- inaccessible or discriminatory experience;
- creator, partner, employee, or community misconduct;
- account compromise, impersonation, or infringement;
- harmful placement or public response;
- email, form, routing, checkout, billing, or tracking failure.

Use:

```text
detect -> stop or contain -> preserve evidence -> assess impact
-> assign authority -> correct and support -> notify where required
-> monitor -> review cause -> prevent recurrence
```

Name monitoring, pause, correction, refund, customer support, legal, security, platform, and public-response owners before high-reach work. Correct the record in the same places where the error appeared when practical.

The incident review should capture what happened, affected people and systems, time line, decisions, remediation, remaining risk, and control changes. Do not prioritize reputation language over customer protection and factual correction.
