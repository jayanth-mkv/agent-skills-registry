## 1. Goal routing

| Goal or problem | Diagnose first | Typical strategic focus | Essential guardrail |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| New product/category launch | Readiness, priority segment, proof, distribution access | Sequenced audience rollout, education, proof, capture, follow-up | Do not manufacture news or promise unavailable features |
| More qualified demand | Segment access, intent, message, sales capacity | One primary demand engine plus capture and nurture | Lead quality, follow-up time, unit economics |
| Weak conversion | Audience mix, promise continuity, friction, proof, offer | Repair the path and test the largest plausible blocker | Downstream quality and accessibility |
| Low activation | Acquisition promise, first value, onboarding, product friction | Reduce time/effort to experienced value | Retention and support burden |
| Churn or low repeat use | Cohorts, value realization, expectation, service failures | Repair value delivery, habit/use case, risk detection, win-back | Complaint, discount dependence, margin |
| Expansion/revenue | Segment value, adoption depth, pricing/packaging, buying triggers | Value-based expansion path and timely offer | Customer outcome and cannibalization |
| Brand/positioning | Audience understanding, category, differentiation, memory, proof | Distinct position and consistent evidence across touchpoints | Avoid unprovable or exclusionary claims |
| Content/SEO/discovery | Demand, intent, authority, site/product connection, distribution | Topic/format system tied to journeys and owned value | Quality, originality, conversion, maintenance |
| Email/lifecycle | Relationship, states, triggers, consent, cross-channel journey | Behavior-led program with branches and exits | Complaints, fatigue, privacy, downstream value |
| Paid growth | Conversion, retention, margin, tracking, creative supply | Bounded audience-message-offer tests before scale | Incrementality, payback, budget caps |
| Local, app, or marketplace discovery | Eligibility, listing truth, customer intent, destination, product/service readiness | Accurate presence, native creative, review and activation system | Policy, review integrity, downstream quality |
| Outbound or account-based growth | Fit, demand signal, contact basis, sales capacity, deal value | Narrow account play with useful outreach and reliable routing | Objections, suppression, data rights, customer fit |
| Creator, community, referral, or partnership growth | Delivered value, audience overlap, trust, operating owner | Mutual-value program with clear incentives, rights, and handoffs | Disclosure, fraud, member/partner value |
| PR, event, or public launch | News or live value, readiness, proof, follow-up | Sequenced story or experience connected to customer progress | Truth, accessibility, support capacity, correction path |
| New segment/market | Problem intensity, access, localization, operational fit | Beachhead segment and evidence-building motion | Serviceability, cultural/legal fit |

Use the full workflow for expensive or cross-functional choices. For a tactical deliverable, state the relevant assumptions and create it.

## 2. Diagnosis memo

```markdown
# Marketing diagnosis
## Decision to make
## Executive finding
## Evidence and baseline
## Journey map and primary constraint
## Rival explanations
## Recommended action
## Fastest discriminating tests
## Measurement gaps
## Risks and unknowns
## Next action, owner, and date
```

Write findings as:

```text
evidence -> interpretation -> impact -> recommendation -> confidence
```

## 3. One-page strategy

```markdown
# Marketing strategy
## Outcome and horizon
## Priority audience, trigger, job, and disqualifiers
## Current state and binding constraint
## Position, promise, differentiation, and proof
## Strategic approach
## Offer and customer journey
## Primary engine, support channels, and experiments
## Metrics, guardrails, and learning agenda
## Capabilities, budget range, and dependencies
## Non-goals, assumptions, risks, and stop conditions
## Immediate next actions
```

Keep one page truly selective. Link to research, channel, campaign, and measurement briefs for detail.

## 4. Full marketing plan

Use a full plan only when the user needs a cross-functional operating document. Keep sections modular and omit unsupported filler.

```markdown
# Marketing plan
## Executive recommendation and decisions required
## Scope, planning horizon, and definition of success
## Business model, current state, baselines, and economics
## Market, customer, competitor, and category evidence
## Customer journey and binding constraint
## Priority audience, trigger, job, and disqualifiers
## Position, messages, proof, and brand implications
## Offer, pricing, purchase path, and delivered value
## Objectives, metric tree, guardrails, and assumptions
## Primary engine, supporting channels, and experiments
## Campaign, content, creative, discovery, and distribution programs
## Sales, onboarding, lifecycle, retention, expansion, and advocacy
## Roadmap, owners, dependencies, team, and capability gaps
## Budget scenarios, forecast ranges, and capacity
## Measurement, data repair, experiments, and decision cadence
## Operations, approvals, accessibility, privacy, risk, and incident controls
## Non-goals, stop conditions, fallback scenarios, and immediate actions
```

Separate current facts, verified findings, inference, assumptions, and unknowns. Link detailed briefs rather than duplicating them. Make later work conditional on evidence; do not fill an annual plan with invented precision.

## 7. Channel and content plans

Channel brief:

```markdown
## Channel job in the strategy
## Audience behavior and native context
## Offer, message, and format
## Destination and follow-up
## Operating cadence and content/creative supply
## Owner, workflow, and capacity
## Metrics, economics, and decision rules
## Platform, privacy, consent, and concentration risks
```

Content plan table:

| Content unit | Audience/job | Journey job | Evidence/angle | Format | Distribution | Destination/CTA | Owner/date | Metric | Repurpose/maintain |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

Do not create a calendar until the strategic themes, distribution, capacity, and destination are clear.

## 8. Lifecycle program

```markdown
# Lifecycle program
## Audience relationship and expected value
## State model
## Entry, eligibility, and exclusions
## Program goal and success behavior
## Message map: trigger -> job -> action -> next condition
## Branches, suppressions, frequency, and exits
## Cross-channel/product/sales/support coordination
## Sender, reply, preferences, consent, and compliance review
## Events, metrics, holdout/test, and guardrails
## Build, QA, launch, monitoring, and sunset owner
```

For each message include subject, preview, body, CTA, destination, timing condition, and instrumentation when copy is requested.

## 10. Review and handoff

End every substantial deliverable with:

- **Recommendation:** one clear choice.
- **Why:** evidence and constraint fit.
- **Assumptions/unknowns:** what is not established.
- **Not now:** rejected work and rationale.
- **Next action:** one owner, output, and date.
- **Decision checkpoint:** evidence expected and resulting choice.
- **Verification:** current facts, laws, provider policies, benchmarks, or technical behavior still requiring confirmation.

Use direct language. Name tradeoffs. Avoid inflated certainty, generic “best practices,” and plans whose only action is more planning.
