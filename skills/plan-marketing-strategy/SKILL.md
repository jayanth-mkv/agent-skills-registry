# Plan Marketing Strategy

Turn a goal or marketing problem into the smallest coherent strategy that can produce evidence and results. Diagnose before prescribing, then help execute the work the user actually needs.

Use this original outcome-to-learning loop:

```text
outcome -> evidence -> constraint -> strategic choices -> customer journey
-> execution -> measurement -> learning -> next decision
```

Enter at the stage the user needs. Preserve the links between stages so a tactical deliverable still serves a real outcome.

## Operating principles

- Start from the business outcome, audience behavior, and binding constraint—not a favorite channel or framework.
- Treat research, strategy, planning, production, distribution, measurement, and learning as one loop.
- Separate supplied facts, verified findings, reasonable inferences, assumptions, and unknowns.
- Prefer a few connected choices over an exhaustive list of unrelated tactics.
- Match scope to stage, budget, time, team capacity, sales motion, product readiness, and evidence quality.
- Protect customer trust. Reject deceptive claims, fake scarcity, fabricated proof, dark patterns, unwanted outreach, review manipulation, and platform-rule evasion.
- Verify current laws, platform rules, pricing, benchmarks, and market facts from primary sources when they affect the recommendation.
- Do not publish, send, enroll contacts, change production systems, or commit spend unless the user authorized that action.
- Do not require a persistent context file, state machine, special runtime, or integration. Create reusable working documents only when they help the requested task.

## Route the request

Choose the lightest mode that resolves the request:

| Request | Mode | Default result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| “What should we do?” or a broad goal | Strategy | Diagnosis, choices, strategy, roadmap |
| “Why is this underperforming?” | Diagnosis | Evidence map, constraint, rival causes, next tests |
| “Plan this launch/campaign/channel” | Planning | Brief, journey, work plan, metrics, risks |
| “Write/build the assets” | Execution | Finished artifacts plus assumptions and QA |
| “Review this plan/campaign” | Audit | Evidence-backed gaps and prioritized repairs |
| “Give me ideas” | Exploration | Diverse options filtered and ranked for fit |
| “Organize our marketing work” | Operations | Ownership, workflow, governance, cadence, decision gates |

For a small tactical request, perform a quick fit check and do the work. Do not force a full strategy exercise. For a broad or high-cost request, use the complete workflow.

## Execute the core workflow

### 1. Frame the decision

Restate the task as a decision:

```text
Given [current state], how should [organization] move [priority audience]
from [current behavior] to [desired behavior] in order to achieve [business outcome],
within [time, budget, capacity, brand, legal, and technical constraints]?
```

Identify the requested deliverable, decision owner, horizon, and cost of being wrong. If the user gave enough context, proceed. Ask only questions whose answers would materially change the work; otherwise label assumptions and create a useful first pass.

### 2. Build an evidence ledger

Inspect supplied artifacts and relevant available sources before inventing strategy. Record:

| Claim or input | Status | Source | Recency | Decision affected |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Audience pain | Supplied / verified / inferred / unknown | Interview, analytics, page, CRM, research | Date or unknown | Positioning |

Prefer behavioral and first-party evidence over opinions, and direct customer language over imagined copy. Do not treat competitor repetition as customer demand. Use the research and positioning reference for evidence hierarchy and adaptive intake.

### 3. Diagnose the constraint

Map the relevant customer path without assuming a single SaaS funnel:

```text
reachable audience -> attention -> qualified interest -> valued action
-> purchase/commitment -> successful use -> retained value -> expansion/advocacy
```

Find the narrowest stage that currently limits the outcome. Test these constraint families:

- **Market and audience** — wrong segment, weak demand, poor timing, unreachable buyers.
- **Position and trust** — unclear category, weak relevance, undifferentiated promise, insufficient proof.
- **Reach and distribution** — too little qualified attention or channel-message mismatch.
- **Journey and conversion** — friction, inconsistent message, weak offer, unclear next action.
- **Activation and delivery** — acquisition promise does not become experienced value.
- **Retention and economics** — churn, low repeat use, weak margins, long payback, poor expansion.
- **Capability and operations** — inadequate capacity, sales follow-up, content supply, tooling, or ownership.
- **Measurement** — broken definitions, missing baselines, biased samples, or attribution blind spots.

Do not optimize a later-stage rate when an earlier-stage quality or product problem dominates it. State the primary diagnosis, confidence, evidence, rival explanations, and what would disprove it.

### 4. Define the objective and guardrails

Set one primary outcome for the planning horizon. Connect it to an observable audience behavior and a baseline:

```text
Business outcome -> audience behavior -> leading signal -> controllable inputs
                                      -> guardrails
```

Use ranges or scenarios when inputs are uncertain. Never backsolve a forecast from invented conversion rates. Define non-goals so the plan can say no.

### 5. Make the strategy choices

Write a strategy contract:

1. **Outcome** — the business result and horizon.
2. **Priority audience** — selected segment, trigger, job, and disqualifiers.
3. **Behavior change** — what the audience should do differently.
4. **Constraint** — the diagnosed obstacle and evidence.
5. **Position** — frame of reference, promise, differentiation, and proof.
6. **Approach** — the coherent way to overcome the constraint.
7. **Offer and path** — value exchange, next step, destination, and follow-up.
8. **Channel portfolio** — one primary engine, supporting channels, and limited experiments.
9. **Measurement** — outcome, leading indicators, guardrails, and learning agenda.
10. **Tradeoffs** — what will not be done, key assumptions, risks, and stop conditions.

Strategy is the set of connected choices and exclusions. “Post more,” “do SEO,” or a long tactic inventory is not a strategy.

### 6. Select channels and design the journey

Evaluate channels against audience access, message/format fit, buying motion, economics, time-to-signal, team capability, control, compounding potential, and policy risk. Distinguish:

- **Engine** — the main repeatable path expected to create the outcome.
- **Support** — channels that strengthen the engine or capture its demand.
- **Experiment** — a bounded test with a hypothesis and decision rule.

Map the complete journey:

```text
source -> message -> asset/experience -> destination -> value exchange
-> capture or action -> follow-up -> sales/product handoff -> retained value
```

Every step needs an owner, transition rule, instrumentation, and failure response. Read the channel reference before prescribing platform-specific tactics.

### 7. Turn strategy into executable work

Sequence work in dependency order:

1. **Repair** — fix broken measurement, offer, trust, journey, or handoff.
2. **Prove** — run the smallest credible test of the strategic premise.
3. **Build** — create the repeatable campaign, content, lifecycle, or sales motion.
4. **Scale** — add spend, automation, cadence, segments, or channels only after evidence.

For every initiative specify:

- intended outcome and audience;
- rationale tied to the diagnosed constraint;
- owner and collaborators;
- inputs, dependencies, and concrete deliverables;
- start, checkpoint, and decision date;
- effort or budget range;
- metric, baseline, target range, and guardrail;
- assumption being tested;
- continue, change, stop, or scale rule.

Use now/next/later or 30/60/90-day horizons unless a different cadence fits. Do not fill a 12-month plan with false precision; keep later horizons conditional.

### 8. Produce or improve the assets

When execution is requested, create the actual research synthesis, positioning, brand or creative brief, copy, website or discovery specification, offer, pricing hypothesis, campaign map, paid-media plan, content or social plan, community/partner program, outreach or email sequence, lifecycle flow, landing-page outline, sales handoff, operating plan, measurement specification, or experiment cards. Keep every asset consistent with the strategy contract.

For each claim, preserve verified facts and mark placeholders. For each CTA, specify the destination and next experience. For each content unit, specify its audience, job, distribution, repurposing, and success signal. For each lifecycle message, specify trigger, eligibility, suppression, branch, exit, and goal.

If `analyze-email-effectiveness` is installed, use it for a deep audit of existing email artifacts; this skill remains sufficient for planning when it is not installed.

### 9. Validate the plan

Run a red-team pass:

- **Evidence:** Which recommendations rely on facts, inference, or untested assumptions?
- **Coherence:** Do audience, message, offer, channel, destination, and follow-up match?
- **Focus:** Is there a clear primary bet and an explicit not-now list?
- **Feasibility:** Can the named team execute the volume and quality within constraints?
- **Economics:** Could this work at plausible conversion, margin, payback, and capacity ranges?
- **Measurement:** Are events, denominators, windows, baselines, and decision rules defined?
- **Customer value:** Does the plan help the audience make real progress?
- **Trust and safety:** Are consent, accessibility, privacy, claims, brand, platform, and legal risks addressed?
- **Failure design:** Are leading warnings, stop rules, and fallback actions present?

Revise material failures before handoff.

## Handoff

Lead with the recommendation, not the process. Deliver:

- the decision and why it fits;
- evidence, assumptions, unknowns, and confidence;
- audience, constraint, position, and strategy contract;
- prioritized initiatives and explicit non-goals;
- owners, dependencies, timing, budget/effort ranges, and next action;
- metric definitions, instrumentation gaps, tests, and decision rules;
- risks, live facts that still need verification, and what would change the plan.

Match depth to the request. A tactical answer may need one page; a cross-functional strategy may need a linked set of briefs. Never use length as a substitute for choice.
